01 · Abstract & Overview

Abstract & Overview

This project evaluates a Support Vector Machine with an RBF kernel on a custom synthetic regional dataset of 569 records across six Jharkhand regions. Five features — age, tumor size, lymph-node status, family history and BMI — are used to classify benign and malignant cases. SVM is compared with a Decision Tree baseline.

03 · SVM Theory

SVM Theory — the RBF hyperplane

Maximum margin

An SVM picks the boundary that leaves the widest possible street between classes. Only the points on the street edge — the support vectors — define it.

Kernel trick

The RBF kernel measures similarity as exp(−γ‖xᵢ−xⱼ‖²), letting a linear separator in feature space become a curved surface in the original space.

C and γ

C trades margin width for training errors (soft → hard). γ sets how local each support vector's influence is, and so how wiggly the surface gets.

K(xᵢ, xⱼ) = exp(−γ‖xᵢ − xⱼ‖²)γ = 1.00

09 · Complexity Analysis

Complexity Analysis

Kernel SVM training scales between O(n²) and O(n³) in the number of samples; A Decision Tree is approximately O(n·d·log n). At n = 569 both models are small enough for a quick experiment; larger datasets may change the computational trade-offs.

Training

SVC training can range from roughly O(n²) to O(n³), depending on data and solver behavior. DT training is commonly around O(n·d·log n).

Prediction

SVM: O(n_SV·d) — cost grows with support vectors. DT: O(depth).

Memory

Kernel cache up to O(n²). With 569 records that is only ~2.6 MB of float64.

10 · Limitations & Future Scope

Limitations & Future Scope

Limitations

  • Synthetic regional data, 569 records — limited generalisation.
  • Five tabular features; no imaging or histopathology.
  • Single train/test split, no external validation.
  • RBF-SVM is hard to interpret clinically.
  • False negatives (4 of 44 malignant test records) are important to monitor.

Future scope

  • Multi-centre data across eastern India.
  • Cross-validation, calibration and threshold tuning to cut FNs.
  • Gradient boosting and ensemble baselines.
  • Mammogram / ultrasound features with CNNs.
  • SHAP-style explanations for clinicians.

Ethics & deployment

  • Decision support, never a replacement for a clinician.
  • Audit performance across age and district subgroups.
  • Privacy-preserving or federated training.
  • Prospective clinical study before use.
